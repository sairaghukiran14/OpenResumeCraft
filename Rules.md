

🔍 1. Resume Parsing Rules
Before any matching happens, ATS parses your resume to extract structured data. Parsing failures kill your score before matching even begins.
ATS extracts details like contact info, work history, and skills using OCR (Optical Character Recognition) and NLP (Natural Language Processing). Non-standard resumes with graphics or unconventional layouts may get misread. Scale
What breaks parsing:

Contact information placed in headers or footers — older ATS may not read these areas. Inconsistent date formats (e.g., mixing "March 2023" with "03/2024") can also cause confusion. Scale
PDFs generated by design tools like Canva embed text as image layers that ATS cannot extract. Always generate PDFs from a word processor, not a design application. 22 Skills
Tables, text boxes, multi-column layouts, and embedded graphics all break parsing.


🧠 2. Keyword Matching Rules
2a. Exact Match
The ATS extracts keywords from the job description (required skills, preferred skills, job title, certifications, tools) and checks how many appear in your resume. Key rule: Mirror the job description's exact language where possible. "Project Management" and "project mgmt" may score differently in older systems. Resume Optimizer Pro
2b. Fuzzy / Semantic Match (Modern ATS)
After parsing, the ATS identifies key skills and terms from the job description and checks for both exact matches and related phrases. For example, if a job posting lists "Python programming," the ATS will look for "Python," "Python development," or "Python scripting." It also recognizes phrases like "managed projects" or "led initiatives" as relevant matches for "project management." Scale
Modern ATS platforms use NLP models (BERT, RoBERTa, or proprietary embeddings) to understand meaning, not just characters. The system converts both the job description and your resume into numerical vectors and measures how close they are in "meaning space." This allows the system to recognize that "led a cross-functional team" and "project management" describe overlapping competencies, or that "revenue growth" and "sales increase" are semantically related. 22 Skills
2c. Abbreviation Rule
Always include the full term + acronym — for example, "Customer Relationship Management (CRM)" — as inconsistent abbreviations hurt your score. Careerlaunchcampus

⚖️ 3. Keyword Weighting & Placement Rules
Not all keyword locations are equal. Placement matters significantly.
Terms in your Skills section and professional summary receive the highest weight in most ATS algorithms. Terms in job description bullets score lower. RecruitBPM
Critical skills should appear in multiple locations: summary, skills section, and relevant bullets. High-priority keywords should appear at the top of your resume, not just in one section. Resume Optimizer Pro
Priority hierarchy (highest to lowest weight):

Professional Summary / Objective
Skills Section
Job Titles
Recent Work Experience bullets
Older experience bullets


📊 4. Keyword Density Rules
Keyword density is how often a specific skill (e.g., "Python") appears in your resume compared to the job description. If the job description mentions it 5 times and you only mention it once, your density score is low. Kudoswall
Use A/B tests over 3–4 weeks with 20–30 applications, varying density (approximately 1.5–2.2%), placement (summary vs. experience), and terminology (traditional vs. emerging). Careerlaunchcampus
But don't over-stuff: Stuffing your resume with too many keywords can backfire, as ATS systems are designed to spot unnatural repetition. Scale

🏆 5. Required vs. Preferred Qualification Weighting
Terms listed as requirements carry more weight in ATS scoring than preferred skills. Build your skills section around requirements first. RecruitBPM
The most important keywords are those listed as required qualifications — these carry the most weight in scoring. 22 Skills

📐 6. Section Completeness Rules
Systems evaluate resumes on: (1) Parseability — can it read your phone/email? (2) Completeness — do you have education/dates? (3) Relevance — do your keywords match the job? Kudoswall
ATS expects these standard sections:

Contact Info (in the body, not header/footer)
Professional Summary
Work Experience (with dates)
Skills
Education
Certifications (if required by JD)


🔗 7. Contextual Relevance Rules
Contextual weighting means keywords paired with impact metrics score higher. Career progression logic also applies — keyword usage must make sense for your level. Industry intelligence also matters, as relevance shifts by sector (tech vs. healthcare vs. finance). Careerlaunchcampus
Keywords in context carry more semantic weight than a list. The recommended format is: Action verb + skill/tool + measurable result. Resume Optimizer Pro

🚫 8. Penalty / Disqualification Rules
Common disqualifying mistakes include: exact-match-only thinking (ignoring related terms undercuts semantic scoring), generic skills lists ("Communication, leadership" with zero proof = noise), and hidden text (tiny fonts or white text are detectable and disqualifying). Careerlaunchcampus
Non-linear career paths or gaps in employment can lower your score. Scale

🎯 9. ATS Score Thresholds
A good ATS score is 70% or higher. Excellent ATS scores are 85% and above. Jobalytics
75% of resumes are rejected by ATS before reaching a recruiter — the primary reasons being missing keywords, incompatible formatting, and structural issues. ResumeAdapter

🧬 10. Advanced NLP / Semantic Scoring (Modern ATS)
Advanced systems use NLP techniques such as tokenization, lemmatization, named entity recognition, and semantic similarity analysis to understand the contextual meaning of resume content, rather than relying solely on exact keyword matches. Ijerst
Research shows transformer-based models (BERT, RoBERTa, DistilBERT, GPT, Gemini, Llama) using cosine similarity for embedding comparison significantly outperform conventional ATS systems — up to 15–16% improvement in candidate ranking accuracy. MDPI
